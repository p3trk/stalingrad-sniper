62nd Army · Sniper detachment · Order No. 17

Stalingrad Sniper

Red October factory district · 12 November 1942 · 07:40

Situation

Every morning a German battalion officer walks from his headquarters to an observation post behind the forward trench. He directs the machine guns that have pinned our company for a week. Your orders: get into no-man’s-land unseen, choose a hide, kill him, and come back alive. Pvt. Orlov crawls with you as spotter.

Sniper’s field notebook

Fieldcraft

Stalking

  1. Movement gives you away first. Shape, silhouette, shine and sound follow. Crawl anywhere an enemy might be looking, and freeze when a patrol turns your way.
  2. Use what grows and what was shelled. Tall grass, bushes and craters break up your outline. Open mud does not.
  3. Running is loud. Patrols hear boots inside 50 m even when they are facing away.

Choosing a hide

  1. Field of fire. You must see the target area while lying down. Assess before you settle.
  2. Avoid landmarks. A lone tree, a burnt-out tank, a haystack: enemy observers and mortar crews look there first.
  3. Stay off the skyline. Lie on the forward slope a little below the crest (the “military crest”), never on top of it.
  4. Keep the sun behind you. Looking into a low sun makes your scope lens glint.
  5. Plan the way out. A covered withdrawal route behind the hide lets you displace after shooting.

Range

Range (m) = target height (m) × 1000 ÷ height in mils. Use the binocular mil scale. A standing man is about 1.75 m, so a man 4 mils tall is about 440 m away.

Wind

Angle of the flag from its pole ÷ 4 ≈ wind speed in mph. Smoke leans the same way. Wind from 3 or 9 o’clock has full value, from 1–2, 4–5, 7–8 and 10–11 about half, and from 12 or 6 none. A 10 mph full-value wind pushes a 7.62 mm round about 0.5 m at 450 m. Dial windage into the wind.

The rifle

The PU scope magnifies 3.5× with a 4.5° field of view. Its elevation drum is marked 1 to 13 in hundreds of metres. Set it to the nearest hundred and hold a little high or low for the rest. A moving man needs lead: the round takes about 0.7 s to reach 450 m.

Shooting

  1. Breathe. Fire in the pause at the bottom of a breath (hold Shift). Hold too long and the reticle shakes.
  2. Target priority: officers, snipers, machine-gun crews, signallers.
  3. Shoot and move. Every shot raises your exposure. Fire while our artillery is firing to mask the report. When the mortars find you, leave.
  4. Work in pairs. Your spotter watches the impact and calls the correction.
